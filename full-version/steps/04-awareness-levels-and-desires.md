# Step 4: Awareness Levels and Desires

Applies Eugene Schwartz's five awareness levels and a "so I can" ladder to the evidence you collected. Hidden motives are labeled as inference, not passed off as findings.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 15 min |
| **Web browsing** | No |
| **You paste in** | Research Brief, Step 2 output, positioning gaps from Step 3 |
| **You get** | Awareness table, desire ladder table, Will / Won't / Can't Tell table |

## How to run it

No browsing needed. This step works only on the evidence you already collected. Save the output as **Awareness and Desires**.

## Prompt

Copy everything in the block below. Replace every `[BRACKET]` with your own content.

```text
# Role
You are a direct-response strategist who works with Eugene Schwartz's market awareness levels.

# Goal
Turn the voice-of-customer evidence into tables a copywriter can use directly.

# Research Brief
[PASTE RESEARCH BRIEF]

# Evidence
Voice of Customer: [PASTE STEP 2 OUTPUT]
Positioning gaps: [PASTE FROM STEP 3]

# Task
1. Awareness levels. For each of the five levels (Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware), list three to five questions a prospect at that level is asking. Use phrasing from the evidence wherever possible.
2. Desire ladders. Pick the five to eight strongest desires in the evidence. For each, ask "so I can..." three times to reach the deeper motive.
3. Will Tell / Won't Tell / Can't Tell. For each desire, describe what people say openly, what they likely keep private, and what drives them without their noticing.

# Rules
- Tag every cell [Quote] if it uses wording from the evidence, or [Inference] if it is your reasoning. Won't Tell and Can't Tell are almost always [Inference].
- For [Quote] cells, include the link from the evidence.
- Use only the evidence provided. Do not search for new sources.

# Output
AWARENESS TABLE:
| Level | Questions the prospect asks | Tag |

DESIRE LADDER TABLE:
| Desire | So I can (1) | So I can (2) | So I can (3) |

WILL / WON'T / CAN'T TELL TABLE:
| Desire | Will Tell | Won't Tell | Can't Tell |

WEAK SPOTS: desires or levels supported by little evidence.
Format tables so they paste into a spreadsheet with no cleanup.
```

## Tip

Use the awareness table to decide where each asset speaks. A cold ad talks to Problem Aware people; a pricing page talks to Product Aware ones. Mixing the two is the most common reason copy feels off.
